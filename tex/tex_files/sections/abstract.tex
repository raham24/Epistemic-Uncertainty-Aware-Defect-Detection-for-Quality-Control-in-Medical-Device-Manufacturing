Automated defect detection is a critical component of quality assurance in medical device manufacturing. Its importance has grown as medical devices have become increasingly complex and reliant on semiconductor components. In response to this growing complexity, \acrlong*{ml} has emerged as a promising approach for accelerating the detection process while improving the efficiency of quality assurance procedures.

As \acrlong*{ml} is heavily dependent on the availability and quality of data, researchers and organizations have increasingly focused on collecting large-scale datasets and organizing their content using knowledge graphs. Knowledge graphs provide a structured and organized representation of data, enabling efficient querying and facilitating the integration of information from diverse sources.

While aleatoric uncertainty arising from the data collection process has received increasing attention in recent studies, epistemic uncertainty associated with the model itself remains relatively underexplored in the context of defect detection for medical device assembly.

In this work, we investigate the impact of epistemic uncertainty on the performance of automatic detection systems and provide insights into how its effects can be mitigated. We analyze a simple yet principled approach for accounting for epistemic uncertainty at detection time and demonstrate its effectiveness on both synthetic benchmarks from the literature and real-world medical device report data. The code to reproduce and extend our results is available at \url{https://code-repo.com}.
