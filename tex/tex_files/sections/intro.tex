\IEEEPARstart{W}{e} consider the problem of defect detection in medical devices, which is a critical task to ensure the safety and reliability of these devices. Such defects can arise due to various reasons, including hardware malfunctions, software bugs, or environmental factors. Detecting these anomalies promptly is essential to prevent potential harm to patients and to maintain the integrity of the medical device. \emph{The complexity of modern medical devices, which often incorporate advanced semiconductor components, has made traditional defect detection methods less effective.} As a result, there is a growing interest in leveraging machine learning techniques to enhance the detection process (cf. \cite{bonatti2025expert,huang2026unsuperviseddefectdetectionsurgical,kim2024deep,lu2019realtime,cao2024xscan})

In particular, \cite{shenoy2026uncertainty} introduces a general approach to defect–mechanism–parameter violation hypotheses for surface-mount assembly root-cause analysis from uncertain observations. Using \glspl*{kg} as the source of information, the authors propose a method to detect and explain manufacturing defects. In doing so, they leverage stage-wise three-head \gls*{mlp} architecture that models data uncertainty by estimating defect and mechanism likelihoods and parameter-level risk scores, enabling probabilistic and traceable root-cause ranking. Thus, they are able to detect defects (Head 1), identify the underlying mechanisms (Head 2), and assess the associated risks (Head 3). However, this approach is not without its limitations. In particular, the authors note that their method can be sensitive to \emph{epistemic uncertainty}, which stems from the limited amount of information available during the training phase. This can lead to detection failures and misclassification of defects. This is a significant concern in high-stakes applications such as medical device manufacturing, where misclassification can incur substantial costs and pose a significant risk of serious patient harm. A wrong detection performed by Head 1, for instance, can lead to a wrong alert being raised, potentially incurring substantial costs (false positives) or posing a significant risk of serious patient harm (false negatives).
We argue that it is crucial to address this issue, and, therefore, we investigate the impact of epistemic uncertainty on detection failures in the context of medical device manufacturing and analyze a principled approach to mitigate its effects. Our approach leverages a well principled cost-sensitive classification framework that incorporates uncertainty handling into the decision-making training process, allowing for more robust and reliable defect detection.

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{subsec:contributions}
We summarize the contributions of this work as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We investigate detection failures arising from epistemic uncertainty in recent root-cause analysis approaches for semiconductor manufacturing, and extend it to high-stakes domain such as medical device production, where misclassification can lead to substantial costs and serious risks to patient safety.
    \item Building on prior work, we analyze a simple yet effective approach to mitigate the impact of epistemic uncertainty on detection failures, leveraging a cost-sensitive classification framework that incorporates uncertainty handling into the decision-making training process.
    \item We evaluate the proposed approach through extensive experiments on a synthetic benchmark, demonstrating its effectiveness under varying levels of epistemic uncertainty.
    \item We further validate our approach through a real-world medical device case study and introduce a reproducible benchmark, publicly accessible through Proto-OKN, demonstrating the practical applicability of the proposed method in a high-stakes setting.

\end{enumerate}
