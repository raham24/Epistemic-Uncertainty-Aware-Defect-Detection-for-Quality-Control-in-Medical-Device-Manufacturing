\IEEEPARstart{W}{e} consider the problem of defect detection in medical devices, which is a critical task to ensure the safety and reliability of these devices. Such defects can arise due to various reasons, including hardware malfunctions, software bugs, or environmental factors. Detecting these anomalies promptly is essential to prevent potential harm to patients and to maintain the integrity of the medical device. The \emph{complexity} of modern medical devices, which often incorporate advanced semiconductor components, \emph{may limit the effectiveness of traditional defect detection methods} \cite{huang2026unsuperviseddefectdetectionsurgical}. As a result, there is a growing interest in leveraging machine learning techniques to enhance the detection process (cf. \cite{bonatti2025expert,
    %huang2026unsuperviseddefectdetectionsurgical,
    kim2024deep,lu2019realtime,cao2024xscan})

In particular, \cite{shenoy2026uncertainty} introduces a general approach to defect–mechanism–parameter violation hypotheses for surface-mount assembly root-cause analysis from uncertain observations. Using \glspl*{kg} as the source of information, the authors propose a method to detect and explain manufacturing defects. In doing so, they leverage stage-wise three-head cascade \gls*{mlp} architecture that models aleatoric uncertainty by estimating defect and mechanism likelihoods and parameter-level risk scores, enabling probabilistic and traceable root-cause ranking. Specifically, Head 1 detects the defects or lack thereof, Head 2 identifies the underlying mechanisms, and Head 3 estimates the associated risks. The authors note that their method can be sensitive to \emph{epistemic uncertainty}, which may arise from the limited amount of information available during the training phase (cf. \cite{DBLP:conf/cvpr/Mukhoti0ATG23,lakshminarayanan2017ensembles,kendall2017uncertainties}). The distinction between aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty is well established in the \gls*{ml} literature~\cite{hullermeier2021aleatoric}, and the need to communicate and act on model uncertainty is increasingly recognized as a requirement for machine learning in health-related, high-stakes applications~\cite{kompa2021second,begoli2019need}.

Crucially, this can lead to detection failures and misclassification of defects. This is a significant concern in high-stakes applications such as medical device manufacturing, where misclassification can incur substantial costs and pose a significant risk of serious patient harm. A wrong detection performed by Head 1, for instance, can lead to a wrong alert being raised, potentially incurring substantial costs (false positives) or posing a significant risk of serious patient harm (false negatives).

We argue that addressing this issue is critical and, therefore, investigate the impact of epistemic uncertainty on detection failures in medical device manufacturing and examine a principled approach to mitigating its effects. Specifically, we adopt a cost-sensitive classification framework that explicitly accounts for uncertainty in the decision-making process by allowing the model to abstain from uncertain predictions. This formulation aims to improve the robustness and reliability of defect detection while accounting for the costs associated with erroneous decisions.

In our framework, the task performed by \gls*{mlp} Head 1 is treated as a critical decision point, as an incorrect prediction at this stage may compromise the reliability of subsequent decisions and risk assessment. To mitigate this risk, the model is allowed to abstain when its prediction is uncertain, thereby reducing the likelihood of misclassification and improving the overall reliability of the defect detection system. Although outside the scope of this work, abstained predictions could subsequently be subjected to further scrutiny by human experts or additional automated checks. Such a mechanism provides a more cautious approach to defect detection in high-stakes settings and could further enhance the reliability and safety of medical device manufacturing.

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{subsec:contributions}
We summarize the contributions contained in the rest of this paper as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We investigate detection failures arising from epistemic uncertainty in recent root-cause analysis approaches for semiconductor manufacturing, and extend it to high-stakes domains such as medical device production, where misclassification can lead to substantial costs and serious risks to patient safety.
    \item Building on prior work, we analyze a simple yet effective approach to mitigate the impact of epistemic uncertainty on detection failures, leveraging a cost-sensitive classification framework that incorporates uncertainty handling into the decision-making training process, allowing the model to abstain from uncertain predictions.
    \item We evaluate the proposed approach through extensive experiments on a synthetic benchmark, demonstrating its effectiveness under varying levels of epistemic uncertainty.
    \item We further validate our approach through a real-world medical device case study and introduce a reproducible benchmark, publicly accessible as a \gls*{kg} through the \gls*{protookn} (cf. \cite{protookn,oknregistry}), demonstrating the practical applicability of the proposed method in a high-stakes setting.

\end{enumerate}
